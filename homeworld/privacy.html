Home World — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 4 September 2026

Home World is built for children, so it is built to collect nothing about them. This policy explains exactly what the app does and does not do with information on your device.

The short version

  • Home World does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any data — about a child or a grown-up.
  • There are no accounts, no sign-in, and no field anywhere in the app that accepts personal information.
  • The app makes no network requests at all. Every room, model and word is bundled inside the download, and the app works with the device in Airplane Mode.
  • No ads, no analytics, no third-party SDKs, no trackers, no in-app purchases.
  • Which things a child has explored, and the app's settings, are stored only on the device, and a grown-up can erase them at any time.
  • Spoken names and descriptions are generated on the device — nothing is sent anywhere to produce them.

What Home World stores on the device

  • Exploration progress — the identifiers of the things your child has opened at least once, so the home screen can gently show what has already been discovered. These are fixed identifiers from the app's own catalogue of 35 everyday things; they are not names, not scores, and not anything about the child.
  • Settings — language (English / Arabic / follow device), appearance (system / light / dark), narration on or off, voice speed, sound effects on or off, and whether the welcome screen has been seen. This is app configuration, never personal information.
  • All of the above is kept in the app's own local UserDefaults on that one device. It is never uploaded, never backed up to a server of ours, and never shared with anyone. It travels only inside your own Apple device backup, if you use one.
  • No camera, no photo library, no microphone, no location, no contacts, no notifications, no health data, no identifiers for advertisers are requested or accessed. The app declares no privacy-sensitive permissions at all, so iOS never shows your child a permission prompt.

Speaking the words aloud

When narration is on, the app speaks each thing's name and description using Apple's on-device AVSpeechSynthesizer. No text or audio is sent off the device to produce that speech, in either English or Arabic.

The "thing of the day" widget

The optional Home World widget picks one thing from the app's built-in catalogue using only the current date. It reads no personal data, stores nothing of its own, and makes no network requests.

The grown-ups area

Settings sit behind a parent gate: a simple multiplication question a young child cannot yet solve. This is a soft barrier that keeps grown-up controls away from little thumbs. It is not an account, it stores no password, and nothing entered there is transmitted anywhere.

Your control

  • Settings → Data → Delete all data removes the saved list of things your child has explored — every child-specific thing the app stores. Because the app holds no personal data, there is nothing else to remove.
  • Deleting the app removes all of its local data from the device.

Children

Home World is designed to be used directly by children and collects no personal information from anyone, including children. There is no way to type a name, birthdate, photo or any other identifying detail anywhere in the app, and no way for a child to reach the internet, another person, a store, or an advertisement from inside it. Because nothing is collected, there is nothing to request, correct or delete from us.

Changes

If this policy changes, the "Last updated" date above will change with it, in the same release as the behaviour it describes.
