Common questions

How does Twin find duplicates? Twin compares files byte by byte, so a match is a real, identical copy — never a guess based on filename or size alone. It finds both duplicate files and whole duplicate folders.

What is near-duplicate detection? It's an optional mode (Settings → Find Similar) that also catches files that aren't byte-identical but are close — a resized photo, a re-saved document, a re-encoded audio or video file. Use the sensitivity slider to control how loose the matching is: Strict, Balanced, or Relaxed. It's off for documents/audio/video by default and on for images, since each type you enable makes scans slower.

Are my files actually deleted, or can I get them back? By default, Twin moves files to the Trash, so you can recover them until you empty it. Permanent deletion is a separate action that always asks you to confirm first. There's also an in-app Undo right after a cleanup.

Will Twin ever touch my Photos library or system files? No. Twin never selects files inside app bundles or system locations, and with "Protect Photos library" enabled in Settings (on by default), it will not select anything inside your Photos library for deletion either.

How do I stop Twin from scanning a folder? Add it to the ignore list: Settings → Ignored Folders → "Add Folder to Ignore…". Twin will skip anything inside it on future scans.

Where is my scan data stored, and how do I remove it? Entirely on your Mac, in a local database inside Twin's app container. There's no account and nothing is uploaded. To clear it, delete ~/Library/Application Support/Twin, or simply delete the app.

Can Twin scan automatically in the background? No — as a sandboxed Mac App Store app, Twin can't scan while it's closed. Turn on "Remind me to scan" in Settings for a weekly or monthly local notification that nudges you to open Twin instead.
