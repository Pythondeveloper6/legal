Trove — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 1 September 2026

Trove is a home-inventory app. This policy explains, specifically, what information Trove handles, how it is handled, and what it is used for.

The summary is short because the app is: Trove has no servers and no network layer. It never sends your information anywhere. Everything below is detail supporting that sentence.

1. The short version

  • Trove does not collect your personal data. Nothing you put into the app is transmitted to the developer or to any third party.
  • Your inventory lives on your device, and — only if you have iCloud turned on — in your own private iCloud account. The developer cannot see it, cannot retrieve it, and has no access to your iCloud account.
  • There are no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party SDKs of any kind. Trove asks no App Tracking Transparency permission because it does no tracking.
  • Signing in with Apple is entirely optional. It stores an identity on your device only, syncs nothing, and unlocks nothing. Every feature of Trove works without it.
  • Payments are handled by Apple. Trove never sees your payment details.

2. What Trove stores, and where

2.1 Your inventory

Everything you create in Trove — item names, quantities and units, values, purchase dates, expiry dates, low-stock thresholds, barcodes, tags, notes, locations and the way you nest them, item photos, and receipt or label photos — is saved in an on-device database using Apple's SwiftData framework.

If iCloud is enabled on your device, that same database also syncs through Apple's CloudKit to your own private iCloud database. This is your storage, inside your Apple Account. The developer has no CloudKit access to it, receives no copy of it, and cannot read, export or delete it.

If iCloud is unavailable or turned off, Trove falls back to a purely local store and keeps working.

Used for: running the app — displaying, searching, totalling and reminding you about your own inventory. Nothing else. It is never analysed, aggregated, shared, or sold.

2.2 Photos

Trove imports a photo only when you explicitly pick one:

  • Camera — when you tap the camera button on an item, or open the barcode scanner. Trove does not open the camera at any other time and never records video or audio.
  • Photo library — when you tap to choose a picture through the system photo picker. Only the images you select are copied into Trove. The app does not read, list, or scan the rest of your library, and it never writes anything back to your library.

Imported photos are resized and stored alongside the item they belong to, in the same on-device / private-iCloud database described above.

Used for: showing you a picture of your own item or its receipt.

2.3 The optional Sign in with Apple identity

Trove offers Sign in with Apple. It is optional and gates nothing — there is no feature, free or paid, that requires it. It exists only so you can attach a name to the app if you want one.

If you use it, Apple gives Trove:

  • a user identifier — an opaque string Apple generates for this app;
  • optionally, the name you choose to share at the moment you first authorise;
  • optionally, an email address. If you choose "Hide My Email", Apple gives Trove a private relay address rather than your real one, so in many cases the developer would not see a real inbox even in principle.

Trove writes these values to the device Keychain and reads them back only on that device. They are:

  • never transmitted anywhere — Trove has no network code that could transmit them;
  • never attached to, or stored with, your inventory data;
  • never used to identify you across apps, devices or services;
  • never shared with anyone.

Used for: displaying your name in the app's account screen and remembering that you chose to sign in. That is the whole of it.

Because this data never leaves your device, Trove's App Store privacy label reports "Data Not Collected".

2.4 Purchases

Trove Plus is sold through Apple's In-App Purchase system. Apple processes the transaction; Trove uses StoreKit only to ask "does this Apple Account own Trove Plus right now?" and to show the price Apple supplies.

Trove never receives your payment card, billing address, Apple Account credentials, or purchase history for anything other than Trove Plus. Apple's own handling of your purchase is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Trove stores a small local record of your current entitlement so the app knows you are a subscriber when it launches without a connection. That record contains the product identifier and its renewal date, nothing personal, and it stays on your device.

Used for: unlocking the features you paid for, and restoring them.

2.5 Settings

Trove keeps a handful of preferences in the standard iOS preferences store: your reminder lead time, your light/dark appearance choice, and whether you have seen onboarding. The reminder lead time is also mirrored into a shared App Group container (group.com.trove.app) so the Home Screen widget can read it. That container is private to Trove and its own widget; no other app can read it.

Used for: remembering how you like the app set up.

2.6 Notifications

Expiry reminders and low-stock alerts are local notifications, scheduled by Trove on your device and delivered by iOS. Nothing is sent to a server; there is no push notification service involved and Trove has no push capability. Denying or revoking notification permission affects only whether those reminders appear.

Used for: telling you an item is about to expire or is running low.

3. What Trove does not do

  • No analytics or telemetry of any kind.
  • No advertising, no ad identifiers, no IDFA, no ad networks.
  • No tracking, no cross-app or cross-site profiling, no data brokers.
  • No third-party SDKs. Trove links only Apple's own frameworks and its own code.
  • No accounts on a server. There is no Trove server.
  • No selling or sharing of your information. There is nothing to sell.
  • No health data. Trove does not use HealthKit and requests no health information.

4. Keeping your information, and deleting it

Trove keeps your information for exactly as long as you keep it. There is no retention schedule on the developer's side because the developer holds nothing.

4.1 Deleting inventory data

  • One item or location: swipe it away, or open it and delete it.
  • Everything: Settings → Data → Delete all items & locations. This removes every item, location and photo from the on-device store, and the deletion propagates to your private iCloud copy on your other devices.
  • The whole app: deleting Trove from your device removes its local database, its App Group container and its Keychain items.

Copies held in your own iCloud account are managed by you, through Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage.

4.2 Deleting your Trove account

If you signed in with Apple, you can remove that identity from inside the app:

Settings → Account → Delete Account.

This erases the stored Apple user identifier, name and email from the device Keychain and returns the app to its signed-out state. It takes effect immediately and there is nothing left on any server, because there is no server.

One step only you can do. Because Trove has no backend, it cannot call Apple's server-side token-revocation endpoint on your behalf — that call requires a server holding a private key, and Trove has none. To also remove Apple's own record that you used Sign in with Apple for Trove, go to:

iOS Settings → your name → Sign in with Apple → Trove → Stop Using Apple Account.

That step is Apple's to perform and is available to you at any time.

4.3 Withdrawing permissions

You can revoke any permission you granted, at any time, without uninstalling:

  • Camera and Photos: iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera / Photos, or iOS Settings → Trove.
  • Notifications: iOS Settings → Notifications → Trove.
  • iCloud sync: iOS Settings → your name → iCloud → Apps Using iCloud.
  • Sign in with Apple: delete your account in Trove as above, and use "Stop Using Apple Account" in iOS Settings.

Revoking a permission disables the feature that needed it. It never deletes inventory you already saved.

5. Children

Trove is rated 4+ and is suitable for general audiences. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children, and contains no ads, no third-party content, and no way to communicate with other people.

6. Security

Your inventory is protected by iOS Data Protection on your device, and by Apple's own encryption in transit and at rest when it syncs through your private iCloud database. The optional Sign in with Apple identity is stored in the device Keychain. Trove implements no cryptography of its own and relies entirely on these Apple-provided protections.
