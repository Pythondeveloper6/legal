Common questions

What is Plants World? A spinning globe with eight biomes on it — rainforest, desert, alpine, Mediterranean, tropical islands, temperate forest, meadow and wetlands. Tap a biome, meet its eight plants, and each one has a photo, a short description, where it grows, and a "did you know?" fact. Sixty-four plants in all.

Does it need the internet? No. Plants World never makes a network request — everything is inside the download. It works in Airplane Mode, on a plane, in a car, anywhere.

How much does it cost? Plants World is free, with no subscription, no in-app purchases, and no ads.

Is it safe for my child alone? Yes. There is no way for a child to reach the internet, a store, another person, or an advertisement from inside the app. Settings, credits and the erase control sit behind a parent gate — a multiplication question a young child can't solve.

How do I get to the grown-ups area? Tap the gear button on the globe screen and answer the multiplication question. A wrong answer simply gives you a new question.

Is it in Arabic? Yes — every plant name, fact and label is written in English and Arabic, and the layout flips to right-to-left. Switch language in Settings, behind the parent gate.

Can it read the facts aloud? Yes, using the voices already on your device — turn narration on or off in Settings. If your device has no voice installed for the chosen language, the narration control tells you so instead of doing nothing.

What are the nature sounds? Each biome has a gentle bundled ambience loop that plays while you explore it. You can turn nature sounds off in Settings; the sound stops on its own when you leave a biome.

What is the plant of the day? Each day the app features one plant, the same one for everybody, and it rolls over at your device's own local midnight. No stored state, no internet — it is worked out from the date.

How do I erase my child's progress? Settings → Erase all local data, then confirm. This permanently removes local preferences and plant-visit progress.

Where did the photos and sounds come from? From photographers and recordists who released them under free licences. Every author, licence and source address is listed in Settings → Photo credits, shown as plain text because nothing in this app opens a browser.

Can I use it to identify a plant I found? No. Plants World is a storybook atlas, not a field guide. Never eat, touch or use a plant based on what the app shows — see the Terms of Use.

How do I report an issue? Email apps.mahmoudahmed@gmail.com with the device model, iOS version, and a short description. A screenshot or screen recording is helpful.
