What is Sundial?

Sundial is a chrono-nutrition tracker for iPhone and iPad, on iOS 17 and later. It tracks when you eat, not what — a meal is valid with nothing but a time on it, and calories or macros are entirely optional. It shows your eating pattern on a 24-hour Day Ring, generates evidence-graded insights from published timing research, and can optionally use your sleep to anchor everything to your own body clock. It's free, with no account, no ads, and no in-app purchases.

Common questions

How do I log a meal? Tap to log a meal and it's timestamped immediately — that's all Sundial strictly needs. You can add a note, calories, or macros if you want them, but none of it is required.

What are the different ways to add nutrition to a meal? Four, and you can pick whichever fits: type numbers in by hand, describe the meal in words ("two eggs and toast"), photograph it, or scan a barcode on packaging. Whichever route you use, Sundial always shows you the result first so you can check, adjust, or discard it before anything is logged — nothing is saved automatically.

What does describing or photographing a meal need? Both use Apple's on-device AI, so they need an Apple Intelligence-capable device. Describing a meal in words needs iOS 26 or later; photographing one needs iOS 27 or later. Both are processed entirely on the device — the text or photo never leaves your iPhone, and Apple's cloud AI (Private Cloud Compute) is never used.

"On-device AI isn't available" — what do I do? That message means either your device can't run it, Apple Intelligence is turned off, or the on-device model is still downloading for the first time. You can still log the meal — just type in the foods and amounts by hand instead, or turn on Apple Intelligence in Settings and try again later if the model was still downloading.

What does scanning a barcode need? Just a camera, on any supported device — there's no OS-version requirement. The scan itself happens on-device; looking up the product sends only the barcode's number to Open Food Facts, a free public food database, and only at the moment you scan or type it. See the Privacy Policy for exactly what that does and doesn't send.

Why can't Sundial find my barcode? Most likely because that product simply isn't in Open Food Facts yet — it's a community-maintained database, not every product on every shelf is listed, especially anything less common or sold outside a few countries. That's the normal case, not a bug. You can still enter the food by hand.

How do I read the Day Ring? The Day Ring is a 24-hour dial with noon at the top, so your day reads left-to-right the way you'd picture it: morning on the left half, evening on the right. It shows your eating window (when your logged meals fall), your sleep window (from Apple Health, if connected), and the melatonin ramp in the hour or two before your usual bedtime — the period when eating late works against your body's own signal to wind down.

What do the evidence tiers mean? Every insight Sundial shows is labelled Strong, Moderate, Early, or Contested, telling you how settled the underlying research is — Strong meaning multiple well-conducted studies agree, Contested meaning researchers themselves don't agree yet. Sundial reports what research generally finds; it does not tell you what to do. See the Medical Disclaimer for the full explanation.

How do I connect Apple Health? Go to Sundial's settings and turn on sleep access; iOS will ask you to confirm the permission. Sundial only ever reads your sleep data — it never writes anything to Health, and the permission is entirely optional. You can revoke it any time in the Health app under Sharing → Apps, and Sundial falls back to a sleep window you set by hand.

Why are my fasting targets and countdowns switched off? Sundial asks a short safety screen before it turns on any fasting feature. If you indicated a history of disordered eating, pregnancy or breastfeeding, insulin or another glucose-lowering medication, or an age under 18, fasting targets and countdowns are switched off automatically — your timing insights keep working normally either way. This is a safety default, not a bug; see the Medical Disclaimer for why it's there.

Can fasting features be turned back on? Only by changing your answers on the safety screen, and only if they no longer apply. If any of those circumstances genuinely apply to you, please talk to a healthcare professional before starting a fasting window, whatever Sundial's screen allows.

What fasting windows does Sundial support? 12:12, 14:10, 16:8, and 18:6 — all optional, and all built around when you eat rather than what.

Where is my data kept? Can you see it? On your device only, in a local file. There is no Sundial server, so there is nowhere for your data to be sent to us. No analytics, no ad SDKs, no third-party trackers. The one exception is a barcode lookup, which sends only the barcode's digits to Open Food Facts — never to us. The Privacy Policy spells out every category.

Does Sundial need an internet connection? No, with one narrow exception. Logging, the Day Ring, insights, notifications, and describing or photographing a meal all run entirely on your device. The only feature that needs a connection is looking up a scanned barcode, which sends just the barcode number to Open Food Facts — and only when you use it. Without a connection, that lookup fails, but you can still enter the food by hand.

How do I export my data? Use the export option in Sundial's settings to write everything to a file you can save or send wherever you like.

How do I delete everything? Use the delete-everything option in Sundial's settings. It permanently erases the local data file on the device. It's immediate and can't be undone, so export first if you want a copy.

How do I get my data onto a new iPhone or iPad? Sundial has no cloud sync of its own. If your device backs up to iCloud, a new device restored from that backup carries the data over automatically. Otherwise, export a file from the old device and import it on the new one.

Do I need an account? No. Sundial works fully without one — there's no sign-up and no password.

Does Sundial cost anything? No. Sundial is free to download and free to use, with no in-app purchases and no subscriptions.

Why don't I get notified about a missed fast? By design. Sundial's notifications are limited to a nudge before your eating window closes and a caffeine-cutoff reminder — both scheduled locally on your device. It deliberately avoids "missed fast" or streak-style alerts, which can push tracking in an unhealthy direction.

I'm finding it hard to stop tracking, or my relationship with food feels worse, not better. What should I do? Please stop using the fasting features and talk to someone. In the US, the National Eating Disorders Association (NEDA) is at https://www.nationaleatingdisorders.org; in the UK, Beat is at https://www.beateatingdisorders.org.uk.
