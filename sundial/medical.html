Sundial — Medical Disclaimer

Effective 4 September 2026

Sundial is not a medical device

Sundial is a meal-timing tracker and education tool. It is not a medical device, has not been evaluated or approved by any medical regulator, and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate, or prevent any disease or condition.

Nothing in Sundial is medical advice. Sundial does not replace a doctor, a dietitian, a diabetes educator, or any other qualified healthcare professional.

What Sundial's insights actually are

Sundial's rule engine compares the times you log — when you eat, and, optionally, when you sleep — against published chrono-nutrition research, and surfaces the result as an insight. Every insight carries an evidence tier:

  • Strong evidence — supported by multiple well-conducted human studies or systematic reviews.
  • Moderate evidence — supported by good-quality studies, though fewer or less consistent than "strong."
  • Early evidence — based on preliminary, small, or mostly non-human studies; a plausible signal, not a settled finding.
  • Contested — an area where published research disagrees, and Sundial says so rather than picking a side.

An insight is a report on what research generally finds about timing patterns like yours — it is not a measurement of your health, not a diagnosis, and not an individualised recommendation. Sundial does not know your medical history, your lab results, or your medication, and its rule engine cannot account for them. Two people with an identical Day Ring can have completely different medical circumstances.

The Day Ring is a picture of your day, not a verdict

The Day Ring shows your eating window, your sleep window, and the melatonin ramp before bed on a 24-hour dial. It is a visualisation of the times you logged, generated entirely on your device. It is descriptive, not diagnostic — it does not tell you whether your pattern is "good" or "bad" for you specifically.

Fasting windows and the safety screen

Sundial supports optional intermittent-fasting windows (12:12, 14:10, 16:8, 18:6). Time-restricted eating is an active area of research with real open questions, and it is not appropriate for everyone. Before any fasting target or countdown is shown, Sundial asks a short set of screening questions, and if you indicate any of the following, all fasting targets and countdowns are switched off automatically — timing insights keep working, but the app will not prompt you to fast:

  • A history of disordered eating.
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
  • Insulin or another glucose-lowering medication.
  • Being under 18.

This screen is a safety default, not a medical assessment, and it cannot account for every condition where changing meal timing carries risk. If any of the above applies to you — or if you manage diabetes, are underweight, have a history of an eating disorder even if not currently active, or are otherwise under a clinician's care — talk to your doctor or another qualified healthcare professional before changing when you eat, whether or not Sundial's screen would have stopped you.

Do not use Sundial to diagnose anything

Do not use Sundial — or delay, change, or stop medical care because of Sundial — to decide anything about your health. If an insight surprises you, that is a prompt to talk to a professional, not a finding about your body.

Always seek the advice of your physician, dietitian, or another qualified health provider with any question you have about a medical condition, medication, pregnancy, breastfeeding, or an eating pattern. Never disregard professional medical advice, or delay seeking it, because of something you read in Sundial.

If you are struggling with eating

Sundial is built around timing, not restriction, and it deliberately does not notify you about a "missed" fast or push a streak. If tracking meals — timing or otherwise — is making your relationship with food harder rather than easier, please reach out for support:

  • In the United States: the National Eating Disorders Association (NEDA) — https://www.nationaleatingdisorders.org.
  • In the United Kingdom: Beat — https://www.beateatingdisorders.org.uk.
  • Wherever you are, a local doctor or mental-health professional can point you to appropriate support.

This is a signpost, not clinical advice, and it is not a substitute for speaking to a professional.

In an emergency

Sundial is not for emergencies, and it cannot contact anyone for you. If you think you may be having a medical emergency, or are having thoughts of harming yourself, call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department immediately.
