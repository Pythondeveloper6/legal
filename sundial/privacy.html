Sundial — Privacy Policy

Effective 5 September 2026

Sundial is a meal-timing tracker. It only cares about when you eat, not what — and it was built on a single rule: your data is yours, it stays on your device, and the developer never receives it.

There is no Sundial server. Not a small one, not an anonymous one. There is nothing on our end for your data to be sent to. There is exactly one feature in Sundial that talks to anyone over the network — looking up a scanned barcode — and it is covered in full below, under "Barcode lookups: the one feature that reaches the network."

The short version

  • Sundial does not collect, transmit, sell, or share your personal data. The developer never sees it.
  • Everything you log — meal times, and any optional notes, calories or macros — is stored on your device, in a single local file. There is no account and no cloud database of Sundial's own.
  • Describing a meal in words, or photographing it, is processed entirely on your device by Apple's on-device Foundation Models. Apple's cloud AI (Private Cloud Compute) is deliberately not used — nothing about your meal is sent anywhere, to Apple, to the developer, or to anyone else.
  • Scanning a barcode is the one exception to "nothing leaves the device": looking up a product sends only that barcode's digits to Open Food Facts, a third-party food database — and only when you scan or type one yourself. See below for exactly what that does and doesn't include.
  • Everyday nutrition figures come from a copy of USDA FoodData Central bundled inside the app — looking one up is a local, on-device search, not a network request.
  • Sundial can optionally read sleep data from Apple Health, read-only and on device, only after you grant permission. It never writes anything back to Health, and never sends what it reads anywhere.
  • There are no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party trackers. Sundial contains no non-Apple SDKs.
  • Sundial is free, with no in-app purchases and no subscriptions, so there is no payment information for it to ever touch.
  • You can export everything to a file, or delete everything, from inside the app at any time.

Every one of these exists for one purpose: making Sundial work for you. None of it is used for advertising, profiling, analytics, or anything else. None of it is transmitted anywhere.

Where your data can leave the device

Sundial itself never sends your logged data — meal times, notes, calories, macros, sleep, settings — anywhere. Data can leave the device only in three ways, all under your own control:

  • Your own iCloud device backup. If you back up your iPhone or iPad to iCloud, Apple's normal backup includes the app's local data along with the rest of your device. That backup belongs to you and is governed by Apple's privacy policy, not Sundial's — Sundial does not use CloudKit or any sync service of its own.
  • An export you start yourself. Sundial can write your data to a file so you can save it or send it wherever you choose. Until you do that, nothing leaves the device.
  • Looking up a barcode. This is the one thing Sundial itself ever sends over the network, and it sends only a barcode's digits, only when you scan or type one. See "Barcode lookups: the one feature that reaches the network," below, for exactly what that does and doesn't include.

Apple Health

Sundial can ask for read-only access to your sleep data in Apple Health, so it can anchor your eating window to your own sleep pattern instead of the clock. That is the only Health category Sundial requests.

  • The permission is optional, asked for explicitly, and can be revoked at any time in the Health app under Sharing → Apps.
  • Sleep data is read and used on your device only. It is never uploaded, exported automatically, shared with third parties, or sent to the developer.
  • Sundial does not write anything back into Apple Health — not meal times, not fasting windows, nothing.
  • Sundial does not use Health data for advertising, marketing, or any use-based data mining, and does not disclose it to anyone.
  • Sundial works fully without this permission; it simply falls back to the eating and sleep window you set by hand.

Notifications

Sundial can send two kinds of local notifications, scheduled entirely on your device:

  • A nudge before your eating window closes.
  • A caffeine cutoff reminder.

There is no push service and no server involved — nothing about your eating times ever travels anywhere to produce a notification. Sundial deliberately does not notify you about a "missed" fast; it is not designed to pressure you into logging or into keeping a streak.

Evidence-graded insights

Sundial's insights are produced by a rule engine that runs entirely on your device, comparing your own logged timing against published research. Each insight is labelled with an evidence tier — Strong, Moderate, Early, or Contested — so you can see how settled the underlying research is. Generating an insight does not send your data anywhere: the comparison happens locally, and nothing about your eating pattern is transmitted to produce it.

Describing or photographing a meal: on-device AI

Sundial can turn a typed description ("two eggs and toast") or a photo of a meal into a list of foods, using Apple's on-device Foundation Models (SystemLanguageModel) — the same on-device intelligence behind other Apple Intelligence features on your iPhone.

  • Describing a meal requires iOS 26 or later and an Apple Intelligence-capable device. What you type is processed entirely on your device.
  • Photographing a meal requires iOS 27 or later and an Apple Intelligence-capable device. The photo is read by the on-device model and then discarded. Sundial does not store the photo, and the photo never leaves the device.
  • Both routes run against Apple's on-device model only. Apple's cloud AI service, Private Cloud Compute, is deliberately not used for either — nothing about your meal, typed or photographed, is ever sent to Apple, to the developer, or to anyone else.
  • The model never invents a calorie or nutrient number. It only identifies what food was named or shown; every nutrition figure that follows comes from the bundled USDA database or, for a scanned barcode, Open Food Facts (see below).
  • If on-device AI isn't available — an older device, Apple Intelligence turned off, or the on-device model still downloading — Sundial says so and lets you enter foods and amounts by hand instead. Nothing is sent anywhere to check that or to work around it.

Barcode lookups: the one feature that reaches the network

Scanning or typing a packaged product's barcode is the only thing Sundial ever sends over the network, and only when you do it yourself.

  • What is sent. The barcode's digits, and nothing else. Looking up a barcode sends only that number to Open Food Facts.
  • What is not sent. No photo, no meal description, no log entry, no device or account identifier, no analytics, no location. The request carries the barcode number and a User-Agent string identifying Sundial to Open Food Facts, as Open Food Facts asks integrators to send — nothing more.
  • Who receives it. Open Food Facts, a free, keyless, community-maintained food database — not the developer, and not Apple. As with any request to any server on the internet, Open Food Facts also sees the requesting IP address; that's a property of how the web works, not something Sundial adds on top.
  • When it happens. Only when you explicitly scan a barcode with the camera or type one in by hand. Sundial never looks up a barcode in the background, on a schedule, or without you starting it.
  • If the product isn't found, that's the ordinary case, not an error — most products simply aren't in Open Food Facts' database yet. You can enter the food by hand instead.
  • No other feature makes a network request. Describing or photographing a meal, generating insights, and everything else in Sundial runs entirely on your device, as described elsewhere in this policy.

Open Food Facts' data is published under the Open Database License (ODbL), which carries an attribution requirement; Sundial credits Open Food Facts by name wherever a scanned product's figures are shown.

Where nutrition figures come from

Figures for everyday foods come from a copy of the USDA FoodData Central database (Foundation Foods and SR Legacy) that ships inside the app itself. Looking one up is a local, on-device search — no network request, no lookup, nothing sent anywhere. USDA FoodData Central is U.S. government data in the public domain.

Figures for a scanned barcode come from Open Food Facts instead, as described above — a community-maintained database, so figures there can vary in accuracy from product to product.

The safety screen

Before Sundial turns on any fasting countdown or target, it asks a short set of screening questions: a history of disordered eating, pregnancy or breastfeeding, insulin or other glucose-lowering medication, and age under 18. Your answers are stored on your device only, are never transmitted, and are used solely to decide, locally, which features the app shows you. See the Medical Disclaimer for what the screen does and why.

What Sundial does not do

  • No account, no sign-in, and no password of any kind.
  • No analytics or telemetry SDK of any kind.
  • No advertising SDK, no ad identifiers, no tracking across apps or websites.
  • No crash-reporting service that receives your data.
  • No selling or sharing of personal information — there is nothing to sell, because nothing arrives.
  • No in-app purchases, so no payment or billing data ever passes through the app.
  • No network request from any feature except an explicit barcode lookup, and that request carries only the barcode's digits — see "Barcode lookups" above.
  • No use of Apple's Private Cloud Compute or any other cloud AI service; the on-device food-capture features never leave the device.

Your control over your data

  • Export my data writes everything Sundial holds to a file you can save or send wherever you like.
  • Delete everything permanently erases the local data file on the device. It is immediate and cannot be undone, so export first if you want a copy.
  • Deleting the app removes its local data from that device. Any copy that exists only inside your own iCloud device backups is managed through your iCloud storage settings, not through Sundial.

Children

Sundial carries a 4+ App Store age rating because nothing in it is unsuitable for a general audience, but it is designed and intended as a health and wellness tool for adults. It is not offered as a children's app and does not knowingly target children. The safety screen automatically switches fasting targets and countdowns off for anyone who indicates they are under 18; timing insights keep working either way. Sundial does not knowingly collect information from children — in fact it does not collect information from anyone, since nothing it stores ever leaves the device.
