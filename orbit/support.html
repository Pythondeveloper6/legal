Common questions

Does Orbit need the internet? Almost never. The 3D solar system, all facts, and every activity are built into the app and work offline. The only thing that needs a connection is buying or restoring an Orbit Plus subscription, which is handled by Apple's App Store.

How much does Orbit cost? Orbit is free to download, and the free app stays free: the whole 3D solar system, every planet, moon and surface, the quiz, the explorer passport and badges, the fact of the day, and four activities — Rocket Mission, Gravity Drop, Seasons, and Bedtime calm mode — plus all settings, English and Arabic, the accessibility settings, and the Session Time play limit. Orbit Plus is an optional subscription — US$3.99 per month — that unlocks the nine Explorer Tools: Compare sizes & distances, Real scale, Time machine, Orbit Race, Fit Inside, Moon phases, Telescope, the Build-your-own Sandbox, and AR "See it in your room" room mode.

Is there a free trial? Yes — 14 days free for new subscribers. If you don't cancel at least 24 hours before the trial ends, it turns into a paid month automatically, and renews monthly after that until you cancel.

How do I cancel Orbit Plus? Open the iPhone or iPad Settings app, tap your name, then Subscriptions (Settings › Apple Account › Subscriptions), pick Orbit and cancel. It keeps working until the end of the period you've already paid for.

What happens if my subscription ends? The Explorer Tools lock again, and everything else carries on exactly as before. Nothing your child has earned — visited worlds, quiz scores, passport stamps, badges — is removed.

I already subscribed. How do I get it back on another device? Open the "Grown-ups' corner," answer the math check, open the Orbit Plus screen and tap Restore purchases, signed in with the same Apple Account you subscribed with.

Where does the payment go? To Apple. Purchases are processed by the App Store and billed to your Apple Account — Orbit never sees or stores your payment details. The price sits behind the grown-ups' math check, so a child can't reach it on their own.

How do I switch between English and Arabic? Open Settings and pick a language, or choose "System" to follow your device's language. Arabic switches the whole app to a right-to-left layout.

What is the "Grown-ups' corner" and why does it ask a math question? It's a section for parents with an explanation of what kids learn in Orbit and how to explore together, plus privacy details and a way to reset explorer progress. The simple math check just keeps young kids from wandering in by accident — it isn't a security feature.

How does the explorer passport and badges work? Visiting a world, stepping onto its surface, finishing an activity (like the rocket mission or a quiz), and scoring well all add explorer points. Points unlock explorer levels and badges, shown on the Passport and Badges screens.

How do I reset my child's progress? Open the "Grown-ups' corner," answer the math check, and use "Reset explorer progress" under Explorer progress.

Does AR room mode need any special hardware? It uses your iPad's camera and Apple's ARKit to place the solar system on a flat surface. If AR isn't available on your device, the rest of Orbit works exactly the same without it.

Which devices does Orbit support? Orbit is built for iPad, running iOS/iPadOS 17 or later, in both portrait and landscape.
