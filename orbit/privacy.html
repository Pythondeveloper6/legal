Orbit — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 3 September 2026

Orbit is a kids' solar system explorer built to work entirely on your device. This policy explains what the app does and does not do with your information.

The short version

  • Orbit does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any personal data — about your child or about you.
  • Orbit works offline. There is no Orbit server, no account, and no sign-in. The one exception is buying or restoring the optional Orbit Plus subscription, which is handled entirely by Apple's App Store.
  • There are no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party trackers of any kind.

What Orbit stores

Orbit saves a small set of settings and progress values in the app's local settings on your device: your chosen language, labels/motion preferences, your best quiz score, and your explorer passport (which worlds you've visited, stepped inside, or completed activities for, which power your explorer level and badges). None of this ever leaves your device, and none of it identifies you personally.

Camera use (AR room mode)

Orbit's "See it in your room" feature uses your iPad's camera, through Apple's ARKit, to place the 3D solar system on a flat surface in your space in real time. The camera feed is used live, on-device, to detect the surface — Orbit never captures, saves, or transmits a photo, video, or scan of your room. Camera access is requested only the first time you open AR mode, and only for as long as you're using it.

Speech (read-aloud)

Facts can be read aloud using Apple's on-device text-to-speech. Narration is English only — Orbit deliberately does not use the system Arabic voice — and in Arabic the read-aloud button is simply not shown. No audio is recorded, and nothing is sent off the device.

Subscriptions and payment

Orbit Plus is an optional subscription, offered only behind the "Grown-ups' corner" math check. It is bought and managed through Apple's App Store:

  • Apple processes the purchase and bills your Apple Account. No payment details — card number, name, billing address, or Apple Account details — ever reach Orbit or its developer.
  • All Orbit receives back from Apple is whether a subscription entitlement is currently active. That single yes/no is stored in the app's local settings on your device, like every other setting.
  • Buying or restoring a subscription is the only time Orbit's screens involve the network, and that exchange is Apple's, between your device and the App Store.
  • Nothing about the purchase adds analytics, advertising, or tracking. Orbit still collects no data about your child, and still sends none anywhere.

Apple services Orbit relies on

  • ARKit — for the optional in-room AR mode described above.
  • AVSpeechSynthesizer — for on-device read-aloud.
  • StoreKit — for the optional Orbit Plus subscription, described above.

Orbit does not include any non-Apple SDKs, does not use your location, contacts, or photo library, and makes no network requests of its own — the only network traffic is Apple's own App Store purchase and restore.

Your control

  • Turn off labels, motion, or reading level at any time in Settings.
  • Reset your explorer passport (visited worlds and badges) from the "Grown-ups' corner" — protected by a simple math check so it isn't cleared by accident.
  • Deleting the app removes all of its local data from your device.

Children

Orbit is designed for children roughly ages 6–12 to use, often alongside a parent. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children — there is no account creation, no chat, no ads, and no way to enter personal information anywhere in the app. A "Grown-ups' corner," gated by a simple math check, explains what kids learn and how the app handles privacy.
