Album — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 September 2026

Album turns photos you already have into a laid-out photo book. It is built so that your pictures never leave your device. This policy explains exactly what the app stores and what it never does.

The short version

  • Album does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any personal data.
  • There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party SDK of any kind.
  • Album makes no network connection of its own. There is no server behind it, and the developer has no way to see anything you make.
  • Your photo files are never copied out of your photo library. A book points at the photos already there.

What Album stores on your device

Album keeps its own small database on your device, holding only what you typed or chose:

  • The name and subtitle you give an album, and the captions you write.
  • The order you put photos in, and the style you picked.
  • A reference — an identifier issued by the Photos app — for each photo you selected. Not the photo, and not a copy of it.
  • Your preferences: appearance, language, whether mirroring is on, and whether you have seen the intro. These are kept in the app's own settings storage.

Because a book stores references rather than pictures, a photo you delete in the Photos app shows up in Album as unavailable. That is the design working, not a fault.

This database is local to the device. Album does not sync it, back it up to a server, or put it in iCloud.

How Album uses your photo library

Album asks for photo library access for exactly three things:

  • Reading the photos you pick, so their shapes can be measured and the pages laid out.
  • Drawing those photos on screen as you read and reorder the book.
  • Creating a matching album in Photos — and only if you turn mirroring on in Settings. Album adds the photos you chose to that album and removes ones you took out of the book. It never deletes the photos themselves, and it never touches photos you did not select.

Album writes to Photos; it does not read your library back into the app or scan it for anything.

iCloud Photos

If a photo you picked is stored in iCloud Photos rather than fully on your device, Apple's Photos framework may download the original through your own iCloud account so it can be shown or exported at full resolution. That transfer is between your device and Apple, under Apple's privacy policy. Album itself opens no connections and sends nothing anywhere.

Exports

Exporting a book renders a PDF, or one image per page, entirely on your device. The file is written to a temporary folder and handed to the standard iOS share sheet, so where it goes next is your choice. Nothing is uploaded by the app.

Apple services Album relies on

  • Photos — to read the photos you choose and, optionally, to create and maintain the matching album described above.

That is the entire list. Album contains no non-Apple SDKs, and no code that collects data.

Your control

  • Delete any album at any time, or remove everything with Settings → Delete all data.
  • Deleting Album's data leaves your photos, and any album already created in Photos, untouched unless you ask for those to be deleted too.
  • Photo library access can be changed or revoked at any time in the iOS Settings app. Album keeps working with limited access; it simply sees fewer photos.
  • Deleting the app removes everything it stored on the device.

Children

Album does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.
