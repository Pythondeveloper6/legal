Halo — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 June 2026

Halo is a menu-bar utility that lives in your Mac's notch. It is built so that everything it touches stays on your Mac. This policy explains what the app does and does not do with your information.

The short version

  • Halo does not collect, transmit, sell, or share your personal data.
  • Everything Halo stores — settings, sticky notes, timers, clipboard history, and shelf files — is kept on your Mac, in your user Application Support folder.
  • There are no analytics, no advertising, no accounts, and no third‑party trackers. Halo has no server.

What Halo stores, and where

All of Halo's data lives locally in ~/Library/Application Support/Halo/:

  • Settings (settings.json) — appearance, modules, and keyboard shortcuts.
  • Sticky notes & timers (sticky.json, timers.json).
  • Clipboard history (clipboard.json) — recent text and images you copied, kept only so you can paste them again. You control retention, can exclude apps, and can clear it at any time. Pinned items are never auto‑trimmed.
  • Shelf (shelf.json) — references to files you drag onto the notch.

None of this is uploaded anywhere. Deleting these files (or using Settings → Reset all Halo data) removes it.

System data Halo reads on your Mac (and never sends anywhere)

  • Now Playing — Halo reads the system's current media (title, artist, artwork) to show playback controls, and sends play/pause/next commands. This is read on‑device only.
  • Calendar — if you grant access, Halo reads your next event to show a countdown. It is displayed locally and never transmitted.
  • System metrics — battery, CPU, and memory are read from the system to draw the Widgets cards.
  • Weather — if you enable the weather widget, your chosen location and the current conditions are requested from the free Open‑Meteo service. No API key and no personal identifier is sent.
  • App activity — the Notifications module observes app launch/quit events from the system to build its local activity feed.

Permissions Halo may ask for

  • Calendar (NSCalendarsUsageDescription) — only to show your next event.
  • Notifications — only to alert you when a timer finishes.

You can decline either; the related feature simply stays quiet.

Your control

  • Clear clipboard history, delete sticky notes, or empty the shelf at any time.
  • Settings → Reset all Halo data wipes every file Halo created and returns the app to its defaults.
  • Removing the app and its ~/Library/Application Support/Halo/ folder removes all local data.

Children

Halo does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the "Last updated" date above will change accordingly.
