Nook — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 September 2026

Nook is built so your stuff stays yours. This policy explains, clearly and completely, what the app does and does not do with your information — including Sign in with Apple, which is new in this version. Nook operates no server. There is no backend anywhere in this product; nothing you do in Nook is ever uploaded to us or to anyone else.

The short version

  • Nook does not collect, transmit, sell, or share your personal data — with us or with anyone else. There is nothing on our end to send it to: Nook has no server.
  • Everything you create in Nook — items, places, notes, tags, and photos — is stored on your device and, if you're signed in to iCloud, synced through your own private iCloud account, on every tier, free. The developer has no access to it.
  • Signing in with Apple is entirely optional. It only ties a purchase to your Apple Account for a cleaner restore across devices; nothing about it is transmitted anywhere by Nook.
  • There are no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party trackers.
  • You can delete your data, and your optional Nook account, at any time from inside the app. See "How to delete your data" below.

What Nook collects, and how

Nook collects nothing in the sense that matters for this policy: nothing you enter or generate in the app is ever transmitted off your device to us. Everything below is either created by you and kept on-device, or handled entirely by Apple's own systems, which Nook never sees.

What How it's handled Ever leaves your device toward Nook?
Item names, notes, quantities, tags, places Saved locally in Nook's on-device database No
Photos you take or choose for an item Saved locally, via the camera or the system photo picker No
Your Sign in with Apple identifier, and — only if you grant it on first sign-in — your name and email Stored locally on-device, to show "Signed in as ⟨name⟩" and to bind purchases to your Apple Account for restore No
Purchase and subscription status (Nook Plus Monthly, Nook Plus Yearly, Nook Pro Lifetime) Verified by Apple's StoreKit; Nook keeps only a locally-derived cache of whether you're entitled No — Apple processes the transaction; see "Apple services Nook relies on" below

All of the above, if you use iCloud, also syncs through Apple's CloudKit to your own private iCloud account, exactly like the rest of your Nook data — see the next section. That is the only "leaving the device" that ever happens, and it goes to a private container that belongs to you, not to us.

Uses. Every item above is used for exactly one purpose: making Nook work for you — showing your items, keeping your account signed in, and unlocking the features your purchase paid for. None of it is used for advertising, analytics, profiling, or any purpose other than running the app on your own device.

Sign in with Apple, in detail

Sign in with Apple is available from Settings and is strictly optional — every feature of Nook, including all three paid tiers, works fully while signed out. Signing in does two things and nothing else:

  1. It shows your name (if you chose to share it) so you know which Apple Account you're signed in with.
  2. It binds your purchase to that Apple Account, so restoring on a new device is a single tap instead of hunting for "Restore Purchases."

Nook has no server to send this identity information to, and no code that would send it anywhere. It is kept on-device (and, like everything else in Nook, mirrored only to your own private iCloud container if you use iCloud). Apple's own systems already hold this identity as part of operating Sign in with Apple — Nook does not add a second copy anywhere off your device.

Apple services Nook relies on

  • iCloud / CloudKit — to sync and back up your items, places, and (if you sign in) your account information within your own private iCloud account. This is free for everyone, on every tier — it is never a paid feature.
  • Sign in with Apple — optional authentication, handled entirely by Apple. See above.
  • StoreKit — when you purchase Nook Plus (monthly or yearly) or Nook Pro Lifetime, the transaction is processed by Apple. Nook never receives your payment details, and subscription billing happens through your Apple Account, not through us.

Apple is the only party any of the above ever reaches, and it reaches Apple only because these are Apple's own frameworks running the transaction or the sync — not because Nook transmits anything to Apple on its own initiative. Nook includes no other third-party SDK of any kind, so there is no other party to describe here. Should that ever change, this policy will name the party and confirm it gives your data the same protection described here before any such integration ships.

Camera and Photos

Nook can take a photo with the camera, or attach photos you choose through the system photo picker, to help you remember where an item is. It only accesses the camera or the specific photos you pick — it does not read your photo library beyond that, and no photo is ever transmitted anywhere by Nook.

Retention and how to delete your data

Nook keeps your data for as long as it exists on your device (and in your own iCloud account, if you use it) — there is no separate retention period on our end, because there is no "our end" for your data to sit on.

  • Delete an item or place any time, from inside the app.
  • Delete everything at once from Settings → "Delete all data."
  • Delete your optional Nook account from Settings → Nook Account → Delete Account, if you've signed in with Apple. This removes the local account record and the data tied to it, after a confirmation step, and works the same way on a fresh install while signed in.
  • What account deletion does not do: because Nook has no server, deleting your Nook account cannot call Apple's Sign in with Apple token-revocation API (that call requires a server, which Nook does not have). Apple keeps its own record of the sign-in grant until you remove it yourself, in Settings → [your name] → Sign in with Apple → Nook → Stop Using Apple Account, on your device.
  • What account deletion does not do, part two: it does not cancel an active App Store subscription. Subscriptions are billed and managed through your Apple Account, independently of any Nook account. If you also want to stop being billed, cancel first in Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions, or see legal/terms.md.
  • Uninstalling Nook removes its local data from your device. iCloud copies are managed through your Apple ID's iCloud settings, the same as for any app.

Because Nook never transmits your data anywhere, there is no separate "request deletion from our servers" step to describe — deleting the data on your device (and your optional account record, and your iCloud copy through Apple's own iCloud settings) is the entire process, and it is available to every user regardless of location.

Children

Nook does not collect personal information from anyone, including children — see "What Nook collects, and how" above; nothing is transmitted regardless of who is using the app.
