Peek — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 28 August 2026

Peek is built for children, so it is built to collect nothing about them. This policy explains what the app does and does not do with information on your device.

The short version

  • Peek does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any data — about a child or a parent.
  • There are no accounts and no sign-in. The only thing anyone types into Peek is a child's first name, and it never leaves the device.
  • Child profiles, discovery progress, voice recordings, and app settings are stored only on the device, inside the app's own local storage.
  • The camera is used for augmented reality — to detect the floor so toys can be placed on it. Camera frames are never recorded or sent anywhere. The only picture Peek ever keeps is one a grown-up deliberately takes.
  • The microphone is used only when a grown-up chooses to record their own voice saying a word, and only after that grown-up has read a plain-language explainer and agreed. Those clips stay on this device, can be exported or deleted at any time, and consent can be withdrawn from Settings — which deletes every recording with it.
  • Delete all data in the grown-ups' area erases every child, all progress and badges, every voice recording, and every setting, returning Peek to a freshly installed state.
  • Peek can add a picture to your photo library. It can never read, list, or see the photos already there.
  • No ads, no analytics, no third-party SDKs, and no trackers of any kind.
  • Spoken names and encouragement are generated on-device — nothing is sent to a server to produce them.

How Peek works

  • Camera / augmented reality — Peek uses ARKit and RealityKit to find the floor in front of the device and place 3D toys on it. The live camera feed is processed only to do this; it is never recorded, stored, uploaded, or used for any other purpose. Before the camera is ever switched on, Peek asks in its own words where you would like to play, and offers a cozy virtual toy room beside the camera option — so nothing starts until someone chooses it. If camera access is denied, if the device cannot do AR, if the camera is interrupted mid-play, or if a grown-up has turned on "Always use the toy room" in Settings, Peek plays in that toy room instead. Every toy, game, and word is reachable there; nothing is behind the camera.
  • Child profiles — for each child who plays on this device, a grown-up types a first name and picks an animal picture from a fixed set of emoji. (A child can also be given their own language, so one sibling can learn in Arabic while another learns in English, and an optional age band — "2–3", "4–5" or "6+", with a Skip option — which only decides how many cards or number buttons appear at once. Peek never asks for a birthdate.) This is saved locally so each child keeps a separate collection. It is never transmitted and never leaves the device.
  • Discovery progress & collection — which toys a child has found, which celebrations and badges they have already seen, and whether they found today's word are saved locally using UserDefaults, one set per child profile. This is what powers My Collection, badges, and the parent-facing Learning Progress dashboard.
  • App settings — language (English/Arabic/device default), spoken-names toggle, sound-effects toggle, vibration toggle, the play-time limit, and onboarding state are saved locally using UserDefaults.
  • Spoken feedback — toy names and encouragement use Apple's on-device AVSpeechSynthesizer. No audio or text is sent off the device to produce this speech.
  • Your own voice (microphone) — behind the grown-ups' gate, a grown-up can record themselves saying each word, so a child hears a familiar voice instead of the synthesized one. The first time, that screen shows an explainer describing what is recorded, where it is kept, and how to delete it, and nothing happens until the grown-up agrees; declining is remembered, and the screen is never reachable from a child's screen. After agreeing, Peek asks iOS for microphone permission and records only while a grown-up is on that screen and taps Record — never in the background, never during a child's play, and never while listening for anything else. A take stops on its own after five seconds, and a call or another app taking the microphone throws the take away rather than saving a fragment. Each clip is written into Peek's own private storage on this device (Application Support, which the Files app cannot browse). Clips are never uploaded, never shared, and no other app can read them. On the same screen you can play any clip back, delete one, export copies of them all through the standard iOS share sheet, or delete all of them; Settings → Turn off voice recording withdraws consent and deletes every recording at once. Deleting Peek removes them all.
  • Pictures of the toy scene (photo library) — behind the grown-ups' gate, a grown-up can photograph the toy scene to keep or share it. Peek asks iOS for add-only photo access: that permission lets Peek put a new picture into your library and nothing else — it cannot see, browse, or read any photo you already have. The picture goes to your photo library, and anywhere you choose to send it with the standard iOS share sheet. Nowhere else.
  • What Peek never touches — no location, no contacts, no health data, no calendar, no address book. Peek never reads your photo library, and it never turns on the camera or microphone by itself.
  • No network requests — Peek has no server and makes no network requests at all; it works completely offline. The Privacy, Terms, Support, Contact, and Rate links in the grown-ups' area hand off to Safari, Mail, or the App Store, which is your device connecting, not Peek.
  • Word of the Day widget — the optional home-screen and lock-screen widgets pick a word from Peek's built-in catalog using only the current date. So they can also show the active child's first name, avatar emoji, and discovery count, the app saves a small snapshot of just those details — plus the chosen language and whether today's word has been found — into a shared App Group container, group.com.mahmoudahmed.peek, which the widgets read. That container is local storage on your device — nothing in it is transmitted, and the widgets make no network requests. Voice recordings and pictures are never put there.

The grown-ups' area

Settings, child-profile management, voice recording, the learning-progress dashboard, granting extra play time, and saving or sharing a picture all sit behind a parent gate (a simple multiplication question a young child can't yet solve). This is a soft barrier to keep those actions for grown-ups — it is not an account system, and nothing typed there is transmitted anywhere.

Your control

  • Reset a child's collection at any time from Settings → Reset collection.
  • Rename, re-avatar, or delete a child profile from the grown-ups' area, which also clears that child's saved collection.
  • Delete any voice recording — or all of them, or export copies of them — from the recording screen in the grown-ups' area.
  • Withdraw microphone consent at any time from Settings → Turn off voice recording, which deletes every recording along with it.
  • Turn on Settings → Always use the toy room to keep Peek out of the camera entirely, on every screen, without losing any content.
  • Erase everything at once with Settings → Delete all data: every child, all progress and badges, every recording, every setting, and the snapshot the widget reads.
  • Turn off Peek's access to the camera, microphone, or photo library at any time in the iOS Settings app, under Privacy & Security. Peek keeps working without them — it falls back to the virtual toy room and the built-in voice.
  • Deleting the app removes all of its local data from your device, including every voice recording. Pictures you already saved stay in your photo library, because they are yours.

Children

Peek is designed for children. It collects nothing from anyone, including children — nothing a child does in Peek is transmitted off the device.

The one piece of personal information Peek holds is a child's first name, typed by a grown-up so the right child sees the right collection. It sits in local storage on this device, and in the App Group container the widget reads on the same device. There is no way to enter a birthdate, an email address, a phone number, an address, or a photo of a child anywhere in the app. A profile is only ever a first name, a chosen emoji, an optional language, and an optional age band ("2–3", "4–5", "6+", or nothing at all) that adjusts how much appears on screen at once.
