Eve — Medical Disclaimer

Effective 4 September 2026

Eve is not a medical device

Eve is a personal wellbeing companion. It is not a medical device, has not been evaluated or approved by any medical regulator, and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate, or prevent any disease or condition.

Nothing in Eve is medical advice. Eve does not replace a doctor, a midwife, a nurse, a pharmacist, or any other qualified healthcare professional.

What Eve's predictions actually are

Everything Eve shows you about your cycle — the predicted period date, the phase label, the fertile-window estimate, the pregnancy timeline — is a statistical estimate calculated on your own device from the dates you logged yourself. It is arithmetic on your history, not a measurement of your body.

That means:

  • The estimates are only as good as the data you entered. Gaps, mistyped dates, or a short history make them less reliable.
  • Cycles vary. Illness, stress, travel, sleep, exercise, medication, breastfeeding, perimenopause, and many conditions can shift ovulation and bleeding in ways no calendar model can foresee.
  • Eve deliberately declines to name a phase when it does not have enough history to be confident. When it does show one, it is still labelled an estimate — and it is one.

Do not use Eve as contraception

Eve must not be used as a method of contraception, or to avoid or achieve pregnancy. It is not designed, tested, validated, or approved for that purpose, and no part of it is a fertility-awareness method. Fertile-window estimates in Eve exist to help you understand your own patterns, nothing more.

If you are trying to prevent or achieve pregnancy, speak to a healthcare professional about methods that are actually intended for it.

Do not use Eve to diagnose anything

Do not use Eve — or delay, change, or stop medical care because of Eve — to decide anything about your health. If Eve's numbers look unusual, that is a prompt to talk to a professional, not a finding.

Always seek the advice of your physician or another qualified health provider with any question you have about a medical condition, a pregnancy, your cycle, your fertility, your sleep, or a workout plan. Never disregard professional medical advice, or delay seeking it, because of something you read in Eve.

Pregnancy mode

Pregnancy mode in Eve is a personal record and a set of estimated dates. It is not prenatal care, not a due-date determination, and not a substitute for scans, tests, or appointments. Only your clinician can date a pregnancy or monitor it. Follow their guidance over anything shown in the app.

Sleep, gym, habits and learning

Sleep figures in Eve are read from Apple Health and shown to you as they are; they are consumer-grade estimates produced by your device, not a clinical sleep study. Workout, habit, and learning features are general-wellbeing tools. They take no account of your medical history, injuries, medications, or conditions. Before starting or changing an exercise programme, check with a professional — especially if you are pregnant, postpartum, recovering from injury, or managing a health condition.

In an emergency

Eve is not for emergencies, and it cannot contact anyone for you. If you think you may be having a medical emergency — severe pain, heavy bleeding, a pregnancy complication, thoughts of harming yourself, or anything else urgent — call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department immediately.

Your responsibility

You use Eve, and interpret what it shows you, at your own discretion and risk. To the maximum extent permitted by law, the developer accepts no liability for any health, medical, contraceptive, or fertility decision made on the basis of the app. This disclaimer forms part of the Terms of Use.
