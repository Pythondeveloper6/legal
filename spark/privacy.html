Spark — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 31 August 2026

Spark is a science lab for kids, and it's built to be trustworthy for parents by never leaving the device. This policy explains exactly what the app does and does not do with information.

The short version

  • Spark does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any personal data, from children or adults.
  • Spark makes no network requests of its own. It works fully offline and always has. The only network traffic Spark can cause is Apple's own — the App Store handling a purchase, or Apple handling a Sign in with Apple request. Neither sends anything to us.
  • No analytics, no advertising, no third-party SDKs, and no trackers of any kind are included in the app.
  • Everything Spark remembers lives only in local storage on your device — in Apple's standard UserDefaults mechanism, and (for the optional sign-in) the device Keychain — and never leaves it.
  • There is no Spark server. There is nothing for us to store, because there is nowhere for us to store it.

What Spark stores, and where

Spark saves a handful of small values on-device so the app remembers your child's setup between visits:

  • Which of the 94 experiments have been completed (drives the gold stars in the Collection screen)
  • The chosen language (English or Arabic)
  • The chosen appearance (light, dark, or system)
  • Whether sound and haptic feedback are turned on
  • Whether onboarding has been completed
  • Whether the Full Lab has been unlocked — a simple yes/no flag

All of this is stored using UserDefaults, Apple's standard on-device settings storage, scoped to Spark's own app sandbox. None of it is a name, email, photo, or any other personally identifiable information — it's simply which cards on the home screen your child has already tried.

If you use the optional Sign in with Apple, the identifier Apple gives the app (and a display name, if you choose to share one) is stored in the device Keychain, which is the encrypted store iOS provides for exactly this. It is never transmitted anywhere by Spark. See "Optional Sign in with Apple" below.

Purchases

Spark offers an optional Full Lab unlock, sold either as a yearly subscription or as a one-time lifetime purchase. Every purchase is processed entirely by Apple through the App Store.

  • The developer never sees, receives, or stores your name, address, card number, or any other payment or billing information. None of it passes through Spark.
  • What Spark keeps locally is a single on-device flag recording whether the Full Lab is unlocked. It is not a receipt, not an account, and not linked to you.
  • Apple's own handling of the transaction is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • Refunds are handled by Apple, not by us.

Every screen that can start a purchase or a restore sits behind a parental gate — a multiplication question intended for an adult — so a child cannot reach it alone.

Optional Sign in with Apple

Spark works completely without an account, and nothing in the app requires one. Sign in with Apple is offered in Settings, behind the parental gate, as an option only. It is never required to play, to purchase, or to restore a purchase.

If you choose to use it:

  • What is stored: the opaque user identifier Apple returns, and a display name if you choose to share one. Spark asks for nothing else — no email address is requested.
  • Where it is stored: the device's Keychain, on that device only.
  • Where it is sent: nowhere. Spark has no server, makes no network requests, and never transmits the identifier or the name to us or to anyone else. Apple, of course, knows that you signed in — that is Apple's own record, governed by Apple's privacy policy, not ours.
  • How long it is kept: until you delete it, or until you delete the app.

Deleting your Spark sign-in

There is a Delete Account control inside the app: Settings → pass the parental gate → Delete Account. It asks you to confirm, because it cannot be undone.

What deleting does: it permanently erases the Sign in with Apple identifier and any stored name from the device Keychain. Spark then knows nothing about you at all, and the app returns to exactly the signed-out state it was in before.

What deleting does not do, and what you should do about it:

  • Apple keeps its own record that you used Sign in with Apple with Spark. Because Spark has no server, it cannot call Apple's token-revocation API to clear that record — that call requires a server-signed secret, and a serverless app has nowhere to sign it. To remove Apple's record as well, go to Settings → your name → Sign in with Apple → Spark → Stop Using Apple Account on your device. The app tells you this at the moment you delete, so it isn't something you have to remember from this page.
  • It does not cancel a subscription. Billing continues through Apple until you cancel it in Settings → your name → Subscriptions. If you are subscribed and intend to stop paying, cancel before deleting.
  • It does not erase your child's progress, language, appearance, or sound settings. Those were never tied to the sign-in — they are local app data that exists whether you sign in or not. To remove those as well, delete the app (see "Your control" below).

Requesting deletion another way

Because nothing ever leaves your device, there is no copy of your data for us to hold, export, or erase, and no request you need to send us. Deletion is entirely in your hands, through the in-app control above and by deleting the app. If you have a question about any of this, email apps.mahmoudahmed@gmail.com and we'll answer it.

Sensors and on-device services

A few experiments use device hardware, entirely locally:

  • Device tilt (Core Motion) — the rain experiment reads the device's live tilt so raindrops can slant realistically. This reading is used only to drive that moment's animation; it is never stored, logged, or sent anywhere.
  • Spoken narration (AVSpeechSynthesizer) — Spark can speak an experiment's name aloud using the system's built-in, on-device voices. This is text-to-speech only; no audio is recorded, no microphone is used, and nothing is uploaded. Narration is English only; in Arabic the app is silent, and because every scene is nonverbal by design, nothing is lost.
  • Haptic feedback (Core Haptics) and sound effects are synthesized on the device in real time; no audio files ship in the app and none are transmitted.

No cloud, no tracking

Spark does not sync to iCloud, does not use HealthKit, and does not request your location, camera, photo library, or contacts. It contains no advertising framework, never presents an App Tracking Transparency prompt (because it does not track), and includes no third-party SDK of any kind. There are no outbound links a child could tap into, no web views, no chat, and no user-generated content.

The app ships a privacy manifest (PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy) declaring exactly this: no tracking, no tracking domains, no collected data types, and a single required-reason declaration for reading and writing its own UserDefaults.

Children's privacy

Spark is designed for children roughly ages 4–9 and is built accordingly. It collects no personal information from anyone, of any age, and complies with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and Apple's Kids Category requirements by the simplest possible route: there is no data collection to consent to, no advertising, no analytics, no third-party SDKs, no external links, no chat, and no user-generated content.

Purchases and the optional sign-in are the only adult-facing actions in the app, and both are behind a parental gate. Nothing a child can reach on their own spends money, shares information, or leaves the app.

Your control

Because nothing leaves the device, there is nothing to request, export, or delete from us. To remove the optional sign-in, use Delete Account in Settings (above). To clear everything else Spark remembers, delete the app from your device — its local storage, including completed-experiment stars, is removed with it.

One iOS detail worth knowing: Keychain items can survive deleting an app, so a stored sign-in may still be on the device after a reinstall. Spark handles this for you — on launch it asks Apple whether the stored identifier is still authorized, and forgets it if it is not. If you want it gone for certain, use Delete Account before deleting the app.

A previously purchased Full Lab is not lost by deleting the app: it lives with your Apple Account and comes back with Restore Purchases.
