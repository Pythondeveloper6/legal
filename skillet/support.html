Common questions

How does planning work? Add meals to your library in the Meals tab, then go to Plan and tap a meal onto a day's Breakfast, Lunch, or Dinner slot. Skillet builds a card for each day of the week automatically.

Can I change the meal slots? Yes. Go to Settings → Meal Slots to rename, reorder, or add slots beyond Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner — Skillet adapts the Plan tab to whatever slots you set up.

How does the shopping list get built? Skillet reads every meal planned for the current week, merges and deduplicates matching ingredients, and groups the result by aisle (Produce, Meat & Fish, Dairy & Eggs, Bakery, Pantry, Frozen, Drinks, Other) in the Shopping tab. If you change your plan, tap the refresh button in Shopping to regenerate the list.

Can I share my shopping list? Yes. Tap the share icon in the Shopping tab to export it as plain text to Messages, Notes, Mail, or any app that accepts shared text.

Can I reuse last week's plan? Yes. In Plan, tap the "•••" menu and choose "Copy Previous Week" to bring the prior week's meals into the current one, or "Clear This Week" to start fresh.

Can Skillet remind me about meals and shopping? Yes. Open Settings → Reminders, turn on Allow Reminders, and pick the ones you want: a reminder for each meal slot at its own time, a weekly nudge to plan the coming week (Sunday at 18:00 by default), and a shopping reminder (Saturday at 10:00 by default) that tells you how many items are still unchecked. They are all off until you switch them on, and Skillet asks iOS for notification permission only at that moment — never at launch. The reminders are local: your device schedules and shows them, and nothing is sent to or received from a server.

Can I stop reminders showing my meals on the lock screen? Yes. A notification can appear on a locked screen, so Settings → Reminders has a Show Meal Names switch under Lock Screen. Turn it off and a reminder says "Dinner is planned" instead of naming the dish. iOS's own Settings → Notifications → Skillet → Show Previews controls the same thing for every app.

How do I turn reminders off? Open Settings → Reminders in Skillet and switch off whichever reminders you no longer want — meal, weekly planning, and shopping reminders each have their own toggle, and Allow Reminders at the top turns all of them off at once. You can also revoke notification permission entirely in the iOS Settings app, under Notifications → Skillet. Skillet works exactly the same with reminders off.

Does Skillet sync across my devices? No. This version of Skillet stores everything on the device it was entered on — there is no cloud sync, no account, and no sign-in. The app works entirely offline and never sends your data anywhere.

Does Skillet cost anything? Skillet is free to download, and the free version plans the current week, keeps up to 20 meals, and builds the shopping list from your plan. Skillet Plus is an optional auto-renewable subscription — $2.99 per month or $19.99 per year, each with a 7-day free trial — that lifts the meal limit, lets you plan and copy any week, and lets you share or export the shopping list. There are no ads.

Payment is charged to your Apple Account at confirmation of purchase. The subscription renews automatically unless auto-renew is turned off at least 24 hours before the end of the current period, and your account is charged for renewal within 24 hours prior to the end of the period. If you buy while a free trial is running, the unused part of that trial is forfeited.

How do I cancel? Subscriptions are managed by Apple, not inside Skillet. On your device open Settings → your name (Apple Account) → Subscriptions → Skillet, then tap Cancel Subscription. Cancelling stops the next renewal; you keep the Plus features until the period you already paid for ends, and then Skillet returns to the free tier with all of your meals, plans, and lists still on the device. Deleting the app does not cancel a subscription — cancel it in Settings first. Refunds are handled by Apple, at reportaproblem.apple.com.
