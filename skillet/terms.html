Price

Skillet is free to download, and it is genuinely usable without paying: the free version plans the current week, keeps a library of up to 20 meals, and builds the shopping list from your plan, for as long as you keep the app.

Skillet also offers one optional auto-renewable subscription, Skillet Plus, described in the next section. There is nothing else to buy — no ads, no one-off unlocks, and no other in-app purchase.

Skillet Plus subscriptions

Skillet Plus is an optional auto-renewable subscription. It removes the free tier's limits: an unlimited meal library, planning and copying any week rather than only the current one, and sharing or exporting your shopping list.

There are two plans, and both start with a 7-day free trial:

  • Skillet Plus Monthly — $2.99 (USD) per month, renewing every month.
  • Skillet Plus Yearly — $19.99 (USD) per year, renewing every year.

Prices are in U.S. dollars. Elsewhere, Apple charges the equivalent local price, which the App Store shows you in the purchase sheet before you confirm.

Billing

  • Payment is charged to your Apple Account (your Apple ID) at confirmation of purchase.
  • The subscription renews automatically unless auto-renew is turned off at least 24 hours before the end of the current period.
  • Your account is charged for renewal within 24 hours prior to the end of the current period, at the price of the plan you are on.
  • Renewal keeps the length of the plan you bought — a month for the monthly plan, a year for the yearly one — and continues until you cancel.

Free trial

The 7-day free trial is offered once per Apple Account per subscription group, and Apple decides whether you are eligible. Cancel before the trial ends and you are not charged. If you buy a subscription while a free trial is still running, any unused portion of that free trial is forfeited when the purchase goes through.

Managing and cancelling

Subscriptions are managed by Apple, not inside Skillet. On your device:

Settings → your name (Apple Account) → Subscriptions → Skillet

From there you can switch plans or tap Cancel Subscription. Cancelling stops the next renewal; it does not refund the period you are already in. You keep the Plus features until that period ends, and then Skillet returns to the free tier — with every meal, plan, and list you created still on your device. Nothing you made is deleted when a subscription lapses.

Deleting the app does not cancel a subscription. Cancel it in Settings first.

Refunds are handled by Apple, not by the developer. Request one at reportaproblem.apple.com.

What the subscription does not change

Skillet still has no account, no sign-in, and no server of its own. Apple processes the purchase through the App Store, so Skillet never sees or stores your payment method or any billing detail; all it keeps is a local record of whether your subscription is currently active. Nothing about the purchase is transmitted by Skillet to the developer or to anyone else. See the Privacy Policy for the full picture.

Reminders

Skillet can show optional reminders — for the meals you have planned, for sitting down to plan the coming week, and for the shopping list. They are off until you turn them on, each kind separately, and Skillet asks for notification permission only at that moment. They are local notifications: the app schedules them on your device from data already on it, and nothing is sent to or received from any server.

Delivering them is iOS's job, and it depends on the permission you granted and on your device's own notification settings, so treat a reminder as a convenience rather than a guarantee — Skillet does not promise that a notification will arrive, or arrive on time. Bear in mind that a notification can be displayed on a locked screen; the Privacy Policy explains this and the setting that stops reminders naming your meals.
