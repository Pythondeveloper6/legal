Skillet — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2 September 2026

Skillet is built so your meal plans and shopping lists stay yours. This policy explains what the app does and does not do with your information.

Skillet is developed and published by Mahmoud Ahmed, an independent developer, reachable at contact.mahmoudahmed@gmail.com. This policy covers the Skillet iOS app. It does not cover Apple's own services (the App Store, iCloud), which are governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

The short version

  • Skillet does not collect, transmit, sell, or share your personal data.
  • Your household, meals, weekly plan, and shopping list are stored on your device only. Nothing is uploaded anywhere — not to the developer, and not to any cloud service.
  • There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party trackers.
  • Skillet can show reminders — for planned meals, for planning the week, and for the shopping list. They are local notifications, scheduled by the app on your device. They are off until you switch them on, nothing is sent to or received from anywhere, and you can stop them naming your meals on the lock screen.
  • If you subscribe to Skillet Plus, Apple handles the payment. Skillet never sees your payment method, and it keeps only a local yes/no record of whether the subscription is active.

Skillet's App Store privacy label is Data Not Collected: no data is collected by the developer, and none is linked to you or used to track you.

What Skillet stores

Everything you create in Skillet — your household name, meal slots (Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, or any you add), meals and their ingredients, your weekly plan, and the shopping list Skillet builds from it — is saved locally using Apple's on-device database (Core Data). It stays on that device. This version of Skillet has no cloud sync, no server, no account, and no sign-in. Apart from Apple's own in-app purchase sheet, which appears only if you choose to subscribe, the app makes no network request at all: it works entirely offline, and it never transmits your content off the device.

Your reminder settings — which reminders are switched on, the weekday and time you chose for each, and whether meal names may appear on the lock screen — are kept on the device alongside the rest of your settings. Turning a reminder on sends nothing anywhere; see Reminders and notifications below.

If you subscribe to Skillet Plus, the app also stores a single local flag recording whether the subscription is currently active. It is a yes-or-no value kept on the device alongside your settings, and it holds nothing about you and nothing about your payment.

Sync is not part of this version. If optional sync through your own private iCloud account is ever added, this policy will be updated in the same release, and the data would still be controlled by you and your Apple ID rather than by the developer.

Skillet does not include any non-Apple SDKs that collect data. It does not use your location, your contacts, your camera, or your photo library.

Reminders and notifications

Skillet can remind you about three things, and each one is switched on separately in Settings:

  • Meal reminders — one per meal slot, at a time you pick for that slot, telling you what you planned to cook.
  • A weekly planning reminder — on a weekday and time you choose, Sunday at 18:00 unless you change it.
  • A shopping reminder — again on a weekday and time you choose, Saturday at 10:00 by default, mentioning how many items on your list are still unchecked.

All of them are off until you turn them on.

These are local notifications

Skillet schedules its reminders on your device, from the plan and the shopping list already stored there, and hands them to iOS to deliver at the time you chose. There is no push notification service behind them, no remote-notification capability in the app, and no server of the developer's for one to come from — Skillet is not registered to receive anything from anywhere, and it cannot be sent a notification by us or by anyone else.

This does not weaken anything said above. Composing and scheduling a reminder makes no network request, and it sends no data off your device — not the meal, not the time, not the fact that you enabled it. The statement that Skillet makes no network request of its own, apart from Apple's in-app purchase sheet, is as true with reminders switched on as it is with them switched off.

Permission

iOS will not let any app show a notification without your permission, and Skillet asks for it only at the moment you switch reminders on. It is never requested at launch, and never anywhere else in the app. If you decline, or simply never turn reminders on, everything else works exactly as before — Skillet is fully functional without notifications, and nothing is withheld from you for saying no. You can also withdraw permission at any time in the iOS Settings app, under Notifications → Skillet.

What a reminder can reveal

This is the one genuinely new privacy consideration, and it is worth stating plainly rather than glossing over: a notification can appear on a locked screen, where somebody other than you might read it. A meal reminder names the meal you planned — "Dinner: Spaghetti Bolognese" — and a shopping reminder says how many items are still unchecked. If your phone is face up on a table, that text can be read without unlocking it.

Skillet therefore gives you a switch for exactly that, next to the reminder settings: Show meal names on the lock screen. Turn it off and reminders stop naming anything — "Dinner is planned" instead of the dish. The reminder still arrives at the same time; it simply says less. iOS has its own control over the same risk (Settings → Notifications → Show Previews), and it applies to Skillet as it does to any other app; the in-app switch is there so you can quiet Skillet's reminders specifically without changing how every other app behaves.

Nothing about a reminder is recorded off the device. It is built on the device from data that was already on the device, shown on that device, and then gone. Whether you enabled reminders, when they fired, and whether you opened one are not reported to the developer or to anyone else, because there is nowhere for that to be reported to.

The privacy label is unchanged

Adding reminders does not change Skillet's App Store privacy label. It stays Data Not Collected: a local notification collects nothing, transmits nothing, and links nothing to you.

Apple services Skillet relies on

Two, both of them Apple's own, and neither of them a place your content goes:

  • The App Store, which distributes the app to you.
  • Apple's In-App Purchase system (StoreKit), and only if you choose to subscribe to Skillet Plus — see the next section.

Skillet uses no other Apple online service, and no third-party service of any kind. Apple's part is governed by Apple's own privacy policy, linked above.

Purchases and subscriptions

Skillet Plus is an optional auto-renewable subscription. If you buy one, the entire transaction belongs to Apple and happens in Apple's own purchase sheet:

  • Apple processes the payment. The developer is not part of the transaction.
  • Skillet never sees your payment method. No card number, no billing address, no Apple Account email, no name, and no receipt or order details reach the app or the developer. There is nothing of the sort for Skillet to store, and it stores none of it.
  • All Skillet learns is yes or no — whether an active subscription entitles this device to the Plus features. That answer is kept on the device as a local flag, exactly like your meals and your plan.
  • Skillet transmits nothing about your purchase. The app has no server of its own and makes no network request of its own, so no purchase information is sent to the developer or to anyone else.

Adding the subscription therefore does not change Skillet's App Store privacy label. It stays Data Not Collected: the app collects nothing, transmits nothing, and links nothing to you.

Your control

  • You can edit or delete any meal, planned meal, or shopping item at any time inside the app.
  • You can turn each kind of reminder on or off on its own in Settings, or turn all of them off with the single switch above them.
  • You can stop reminders naming your meals with Show meal names on the lock screen, in the same place — and you can withdraw notification permission entirely in the iOS Settings app.
  • Deleting the app removes all of its data from your device. Because nothing is stored anywhere else, that is a complete erasure.

Your rights

Because Skillet sends nothing to the developer, there is no personal data held about you for the developer to access, export, correct, or erase. Everything the app holds is visible inside the app and under your own control, and deleting the app erases it. Children

Skillet does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.
