Incline for iPhone

Privacy Policy

Effective 1 September 2026 · Published by Mahmoud Ahmed

The short version

  • Incline has no accounts, no sign-in, and no servers. There is nothing to register for and nothing to log in to.
  • Incline contains no networking code of its own. It cannot upload your workouts, because it has no way to send them anywhere.
  • There is no analytics, no crash-reporting service, no advertising, and no third-party SDK of any kind in the app.
  • Your workouts and settings live in a database on your iPhone, inside the app's own storage.
  • Incline writes to Apple Health only if you grant permission, and only if you subscribe to Incline Plus. It never writes resting or basal energy, and never edits another app's data. It can also read a set of Health values, but only for the optional Body trends screen, and only after you allow it.
  • The only thing that leaves your device is the subscription purchase itself, which is handled entirely by Apple. Incline never sees your name, email, or payment details.

What Incline stores, and where

Everything below is stored locally in Incline's own container on your iPhone. None of it is transmitted, backed up to our servers, or shared with us, because there is no server and no transmission code in the app.

Your workout database

Each finished session you save is one row in Incline's on-device database. A row holds:

  • the date and time the session started, and how long you were actively moving;
  • the interval profile — every speed and incline you set, and when you set it;
  • the derived totals: distance, elevation gain, estimated active calories, average speed and pace;
  • the body weight and calorie calibration in effect when the session was recorded;
  • the scientific provenance of the estimate: which published equation produced it, that model's version, and the confidence and validity state of the result;
  • whether the session has been written to Apple Health, and if so, the identifier Apple Health gave it.

Your settings

Your unit preference, body weight, calorie calibration, weekly target, appearance choice, and whether you have finished onboarding.

Small operational values

Incline also keeps a handful of technical values in the app's own preferences store so a session survives being interrupted:

  • a snapshot of an in-progress workout, so closing or crashing the app does not lose a run you are in the middle of;
  • a pending action from the Live Activity (pause, resume, finish) waiting for the app to pick it up;
  • a cached copy of your subscription status, so a paying subscriber is not locked out when the App Store cannot be reached. This is a convenience copy only — the App Store remains the source of truth and is re-checked every launch.

Your body weight

Your body weight is a number you type into Incline. It is used to scale the metabolic equations, which are expressed per kilogram of body mass. It is stored with each session so a past estimate stays reproducible. It is never sent anywhere.

What Incline does not do

  • It does not collect, transmit, sell, rent, or share any personal information.
  • It does not create a profile of you and does not track you across apps or websites. It reads no advertising identifier, and it never shows the App Tracking Transparency prompt — because it has nothing to ask permission for.
  • It contains no analytics framework, no crash reporter, no attribution SDK, and no advertising SDK. The only code it links against is Apple's own frameworks and Incline's own InclineCore package.
  • It does not read your contacts, photos, microphone, camera, calendar, or location. It requests none of those permissions.
  • It does not use iCloud, CloudKit, or any cross-device sync. Your history exists on the one iPhone that recorded it.

Apple Health

Apple Health is optional. Incline works completely without it, and every figure in the app is calculated by Incline itself, not read from Health.

What Incline writes

If you grant write permission and you have an active Incline Plus subscription, finishing a session can write one indoor running workout to Apple Health, together with:

Health typeWhat Incline writes
WorkoutOne Running workout, marked indoor, bounded by the session's start and end.
Active EnergyThe estimated active calories for that session.
Walking + Running DistanceThe belt distance for that session.

Incline never writes resting or basal energy. Its estimate is active energy only, so requesting basal would be both unnecessary and misleading. It never modifies or deletes a sample written by another app, and it never writes anything you did not record in Incline.

What Incline asks to read

Reading is separate from writing, optional, and used by exactly one screen: Body trends, reached from Insights. If you never open it, Incline never asks.

When you do open it, Incline asks permission to read:

WhatWhy
Resting heart rate, walking heart rate average, heart rate variability (SDNN), VO₂ maxShown as trends over time beside your treadmill sessions.
Step count, active energyDaily totals, so a treadmill session can be seen in the context of the whole day.
Heart rateThe heart-rate curve recorded during a workout. A daily average would destroy the shape of it.
WorkoutsSo that “workout days” can include sessions recorded by Apple Watch and other apps, rather than only the ones you recorded in Incline.
Body massSo the calorie estimate can be personalised. In this version the figure Incline actually uses is the weight you enter in its own settings.

Everything Incline reads is displayed back to you and nothing else. It is not combined into a profile, not used to score, rate, or predict anything about your health, and — because Incline contains no networking code at all — it cannot be sent anywhere. It is never used for advertising or marketing, and it is never shared with a third party, because there is no third party and no channel to share it over.

Reading is read-only. Incline never modifies or deletes a sample written by another app, and HealthKit gives it no way to do so with read permission. Declining the read request leaves the whole of the rest of the app working exactly as before: every figure Incline calculates comes from the speed and grade you entered, never from Apple Health.

Turning it off, and deleting

You control Health permission at any time in the Health app: your profile → Privacy → Apps → Incline. Revoking it stops all future writes and all future reads. Anything Incline already wrote stays in Apple Health until you delete it there — read permission does not let Incline remove a Health workout, including one it wrote itself.

Subscriptions and the App Store

Incline Plus is an auto-renewing subscription sold through Apple's In-App Purchase system. The purchase, the payment, the renewal, and any refund are handled entirely by Apple.

  • Your payment method, billing address, name, and Apple Account email are never seen by Incline and never reach us.
  • Apple returns to the app a cryptographically verified transaction indicating that a subscription is active, when the current period ends, whether an introductory offer is running, and whether a purchase was refunded. Incline stores a copy of that answer on the device and nothing more.
  • Apple's handling of purchase data is described in Apple's Privacy Policy.

Live Activities and the widget

The Live Activity and Dynamic Island display is generated on your device from the session you are running. Incline uses no push tokens and no remote updates for it — there is no server involved, and nothing about your workout is transmitted to display it.

Children

Incline is a general-audience fitness-tracking utility and is not directed at children under 13. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Legal bases and your rights

Because Incline collects no personal data and transmits nothing to us, we hold no personal data about you and there is nothing for us to access, export, correct, or erase on your behalf. Your data is entirely under your own control:

  • See it — everything Incline holds is visible in the app's History, Insights, and Settings.
  • Delete a session — deleting it in History removes it from Incline permanently.
  • Delete everything — deleting the app from your iPhone removes Incline's entire local database and all of its stored settings.
  • Apple Health — anything Incline wrote there is deleted in the Health app, not in Incline.

If you are in the EEA or UK, we are not a controller of any personal data through this app. If you believe otherwise, contact us at apps.mahmoudahmed@gmail.com.
