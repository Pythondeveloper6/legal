Pass — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 June 2026

Pass is built so the things you keep stay yours. This policy explains what the app does and does not do with your information.

The short version

  • By default, Pass does not collect, transmit, sell, or share your data.
  • Your cards — titles, notes, details, links, and photos — are stored on your device and, if you’re signed in to iCloud, synced through your own private iCloud account. The developer has no access to them.
  • There are no analytics, no advertising, and no third‑party trackers.
  • Sharing a card with another person is optional and off by default. It only transmits data when you turn it on and actively choose to share a card.

What Pass stores

Everything you create in Pass (card titles, notes, custom detail fields, links, photos and files, accent colors, and pins) is saved locally using Apple’s on‑device database. If iCloud is enabled on your device, this data also syncs via Apple’s CloudKit to your private iCloud account so it’s available on your other devices. This data is controlled by you and your Apple ID; the developer cannot see or retrieve it.

Apple services Pass relies on

  • iCloud / CloudKit — to sync and back up your cards within your own account.
  • StoreKit — when you purchase Pass Pro, the transaction is processed by Apple. Pass never receives your payment details.
  • Sign in with Apple — used only if you enable username sharing (see below), to create the account that identifies you to people you share with.

Pass does not include any advertising or analytics SDKs.

Optional username sharing

Pass includes an optional, off‑by‑default feature that lets you share a card with another Pass user by username. This feature is only available if the app has been configured with a sharing backend, and it does nothing until you sign in and choose to share a specific card.

When you enable sharing and share a card, the following is sent to the sharing backend (a Supabase project operated by the developer) so the recipient can receive it:

  • A profile for your account: your username, optional display name, and the identifier from Sign in with Apple. (Apple’s private‑relay option means the developer need not receive your real email address.)
  • For each card you choose to share: its title and notes, the recipient’s username, and the permission you grant (view or edit).
  • The sharing status of each shared card (active or paused).

What is not sent, for any card, ever: your custom detail fields (labels and values), your saved links, your photos and file attachments, a card's accent colour or pin, its created/updated history, and every card you did not explicitly choose to share. Cards you never share never leave your device and iCloud.

Sharing publishes a snapshot, not a live sync. Edits you make after sharing stay on your device until you choose to send the card again.

You can pause or stop sharing a card at any time, and you can decline cards shared with you. Stopping a share deletes the published copy from the backend. If you never enable sharing, none of your data is ever sent to the backend.

Your session

If you sign in, the credential that keeps you signed in is held in the device Keychain, marked as readable only while the device is unlocked and only on that device — it is not written to app preferences, not included in encrypted backups, and not synced through iCloud Keychain. Signing out deletes it from the device and ends the session on the server.

Photos

Pass only accesses the specific photos you choose through the system photo picker. It does not read your photo library beyond the items you pick.

How long anything is kept

  • On your device / in your iCloud: for as long as you keep the card.
  • On the sharing backend: a published card exists only while you share it. Stop sharing, or delete your account, and it is deleted.
  • Nothing is retained for analytics, because there are none.

Your control

  • Export. Settings → “Export all cards” produces a readable JSON file with every card, in order. It is free and needs no account.
  • You can delete any card, or all cards, at any time inside the app (a card’s menu, or Settings → “Delete all cards”).
  • You can stop sharing a card at any time; the published copy is deleted with it.
  • Delete your account. If you signed in for sharing, Settings → Account & username → “Delete account” permanently removes your username, every card you published, and your sign‑in record. Your cards on the device are not touched by it.
  • Deleting the app removes its local data from your device. iCloud copies are managed through your Apple ID settings.

Children

Pass does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.
