Past World — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 4 September 2026

Past World (عالم الماضي) is made for children, so it is built to collect nothing about them. This policy describes exactly what the app does and does not do with information on your device.

The short version

  • Past World does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any data — about a child or a grown-up.
  • There are no accounts, no sign-in, and no field anywhere in the app that accepts personal information.
  • The app makes no network requests at all. Every civilization, story, photo and fact is bundled inside the download, and the app works with the device in Airplane Mode.
  • No ads, no analytics, no third-party SDKs, no trackers, no in-app purchases. The app is free and contains no advertising or analytics libraries of any kind.
  • The only things saved are which parts of history have been explored and the grown-up's settings, stored on the device, and a grown-up can erase them at any time.

What Past World stores on the device

  • Reading progress — the identifiers of the civilizations and daily-life themes your child has opened (for example ancient-egypt, homes), which "Meet someone" stories have been read, and any bookmarks. These are fixed identifiers from the app's own catalogue of 10 civilizations; they are not names, not personal details, and not anything about the child.
  • Settings — language (English / العربية or Follow device), light / dark / system appearance, whether stories are read aloud, and whether the welcome walkthrough has been seen. This is app configuration, never personal information.
  • All of the above is kept in the app's own local UserDefaults on that one device. It is never uploaded, never sent to a developer server, and never shared with anyone. It travels only inside your own Apple device backup, if you use one.
  • No camera, no photo library, no microphone, no location, no contacts, no notifications, no health data, no identifiers for advertisers are requested or accessed. The app declares no privacy-sensitive permissions at all, so iOS never shows your child a permission prompt.

Reading aloud

When narration is on, the app speaks stories and facts using Apple's on-device AVSpeechSynthesizer. No text or audio is sent off the device to produce that speech, and nothing your child says is ever recorded — the app has no microphone access whatsoever.

The photographs

Past World illustrates each civilization with real museum photographs, not drawings. Every photograph is public-domain or Creative Commons licensed, ships inside the app download, and is never fetched from the internet. Photo credits for every image are listed in-app under Settings → Photo Credits.

The "Discovery of the day" widget

The Home Screen widget picks its featured discovery by doing arithmetic on the date already on your device. It reads nothing else, sends nothing anywhere, and needs no network.

The grown-ups area

Settings, the photo credits list, and any control that could change what a child sees sit behind a parent gate: a simple question a young child cannot solve, which generates a new question after a wrong answer. This is a soft barrier that keeps grown-up controls away from little thumbs. It is not an account, it stores no password, and nothing entered there is transmitted anywhere.

Children's privacy — COPPA and the App Store Kids category

Past World is designed to be used directly by children aged 4 and up. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children, so there is nothing that could be disclosed to a third party. Consistent with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and Apple's Kids Category requirements:

  • There is no way to type a name, birthdate, photo, email address or any other identifying detail anywhere in the app.
  • There is no behavioural advertising, no third-party analytics, and no third-party SDK of any kind.
  • There is no way for a child to reach the internet, another person, a store, or an advertisement from inside the app. Every external link is behind the parent gate.
  • Because nothing is collected, there is nothing for a parent to request, correct, or ask us to delete. Erasing progress in the app, or deleting the app, removes everything it has ever stored.

Your control

  • Settings → Erase exploration record removes every civilization, theme and "Meet someone" story your child has opened, and any bookmarks — every child-specific thing the app stores. The grown-up's own settings (language, appearance, narration) are deliberately kept.
  • Deleting the app removes all of its local data from the device.

Changes

If this policy changes, the "Last updated" date above will change with it, in the same release as the behaviour it describes.
