Money Market — Privacy Policy

Effective 4 September 2026

Money Market is a market stall for children, and it is built to be trustworthy for parents by never leaving the device. This policy explains exactly what the app does and does not do with information.

The short version

  • Money Market does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any personal data, from children or adults.
  • Money Market makes no network requests at all. There is no networking code in the app or in its widget. It works fully offline and always has.
  • No analytics, no advertising, no third-party SDKs, and no trackers of any kind are included in the app.
  • There is no account and no sign-in. Nothing to register, nothing to log in to, nothing to forget.
  • Everything Money Market remembers — every child profile, every star, the money book, the wallet, and every setting — lives only in local storage on your device, in Apple's standard UserDefaults mechanism, and never leaves it.
  • There is no Money Market server. There is nothing for us to store, because there is nowhere for us to store it.
  • The app is free, contains no purchases of any kind, and has no payment code in it.

What Money Market stores, and where

Money Market saves a handful of small values on-device so the app remembers your child's setup between visits:

  • Child profiles — for each child, a name you typed and one emoji chosen from a fixed list of ten animals, plus an internal identifier the app generates for itself.
  • Which child is playing right now.
  • Progress, per child — how many stars have been earned in each mode and tier (Count the Table, Pay a Stall, Make Change), how many rounds have been played, how many were finished with no mistakes, and which coins and notes the child has met in the Money Book.
  • Settings — the chosen language (English or Arabic), the chosen currency (Egyptian pound or US dollar), the appearance (light, dark, or system), whether narration, sound effects and haptics are on, how fast the narrator speaks, whether the "unlock all levels" parent override is on, and whether onboarding has been completed.

All of this is stored using UserDefaults, Apple's standard on-device settings storage, scoped to Money Market's own app sandbox. It is not a document, a photo, an email address, a birthday, a location, or anything else that could identify a person. A child's name and avatar are typed by a grown-up, kept on that one device, and never transmitted anywhere. You are free to type a nickname, a first initial, or nothing meaningful at all — the app does not care what the name is, and nobody but the person holding the device will ever see it.

Progress keys are namespaced by the child's internal identifier, which is why switching children switches the stars: it is a bookkeeping detail on your device, not an identity we hold.

The coin-of-the-day widget

Money Market includes a small Home Screen widget that draws one coin or note a day and, when tapped, opens the wallet inside the app.

A widget is a separate extension and cannot read the app's settings directly, so the app mirrors exactly two values into a shared App Group container on the same device: the chosen currency and the chosen language. That is all the widget needs in order to draw the right money in the right words. No child name, no progress, and no other setting is shared with it, and nothing in the widget is sent anywhere — it has no networking code either. Which coin is shown is worked out from the calendar date by arithmetic on the device, not fetched.

Sound and narration

  • Spoken narration uses AVSpeechSynthesizer, Apple's built-in text-to-speech, to say the name and value of a coin or note out loud in English or Arabic. This is speech out only: no microphone is used, no audio is recorded, and nothing is uploaded. If a voice for the chosen language is not installed on the device, the app falls back to English rather than going silent.
  • Sound effects are short audio files bundled inside the app — a coin clink, a till ding, a soft "nope". They are played from the app itself.
  • Haptics are generated by the device.

All three can be turned off independently in Settings.

The parent gate

A short multiplication question stands between a child and everything meant for a grown-up. Behind it, and only behind it, are:

  • Settings
  • The parent progress report
  • Managing children — renaming, changing an avatar, or deleting a child
  • "Delete all data"
  • The "unlock all levels" override
  • Every link that leaves the app — this privacy policy, the terms, the support page, the contact email, and the App Store review link

A wrong answer produces a fresh question, so the same sum cannot be guessed repeatedly. This is required for apps in the Kids Category, and it is why a child cannot wander out of the app or wipe a sibling's stars on their own.

The app also registers a moneymarket:// link so the widget can open the wallet. The router accepts only the calm, child-facing screens — home, the wallet, the Money Book, and a round of play. No link, pasted or otherwise, can land on Settings, the report, or any destructive action.

The parent report

Settings contains a progress report for grown-ups: stars earned, rounds played, the share of rounds finished with no mistakes, which levels are open, and how much of the money book is filled in. Every number in it is worked out on the device from that child's own saved progress. Nothing is sent anywhere, nothing is compared with other children, and the report is deliberately kept on the grown-up side of the gate — it is written about a child, never shown to them.

What Money Market does not do

Money Market does not sync to iCloud, does not use HealthKit, and does not request your location, camera, photo library, microphone, contacts, or notifications. It contains no advertising framework, never presents an App Tracking Transparency prompt (because it does not track), and includes no third-party SDK of any kind. There is no chat, no social feature, no sharing, no leaderboard, no user account, and no user-generated content that goes anywhere — the only things a child creates are stars, and they stay on the device.

Both the app and its widget ship a privacy manifest (PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy) declaring exactly this: no tracking, no tracking domains, no collected data types, and a single required-reason declaration for reading and writing their own UserDefaults.

Children's privacy

Money Market is designed for children roughly ages 4–8 and is built accordingly. It collects no personal information from anyone, of any age, and complies with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and Apple's Kids Category requirements by the simplest possible route:

  • Nothing is collected, so there is no parental consent to obtain for collection and nothing to disclose to a third party.
  • There is no advertising — no ad network, no interest-based ads, no third-party ad SDK, and no in-app promotion of anything.
  • There is no third-party data sharing, because there is no third party in the app and no data leaving the device to share.
  • There are no purchases of any kind — no in-app purchases, no subscriptions, no payment code.
  • Every external link and every parent control sits behind the parent gate, so a child cannot leave the app or reach a grown-up screen alone.
  • A child's name and avatar never leave the device, and are visible only to whoever is holding it.

If you believe a child has somehow provided personal information through this app, please write to us at apps.mahmoudahmed@gmail.com — though by design there is nowhere in the app for that to happen, we will investigate any report immediately.

Your control

Because nothing leaves the device, there is nothing to request, export, or delete from us, and no request you need to send.

  • Delete one child — Settings → parent gate → Manage children. Removes that child and all of their progress.
  • Delete everything — Settings → parent gate → Delete all data. Removes every child, every star, the money book, and returns the app to its first-run state.
  • Delete the app — removes all of its local storage with it, including the values shared with the widget.
