Daybook — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 July 2026

Daybook is a personal planner that's built to stay on your device. This policy explains exactly what the app does and does not do with your information.

The short version

  • Daybook does not collect, transmit, sell, or share your personal data.
  • Your day pages, handwritten ink, and page/appearance settings are stored only on your device. The developer has no access to them.
  • Daybook reads and writes events in your Calendar app (via Apple's EventKit) to show and edit them on the day timeline — that data never leaves your device either.
  • There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no third‑party trackers.

What Daybook stores

  • Day pages — the typed lines and, on iPad, the handwritten ink you add to a day — are saved locally using Apple's on‑device database (SwiftData), in a stable location inside your device's Application Support folder. Nothing is synced to a server or to iCloud in this version.
  • Settings — your chosen page design (Oxblood Leather, Minimal White, Dark Mode, Kraft/Dotted), appearance theme, page font and text size, zoom level, and which calendars are shown are saved locally with UserDefaults, scoped to Daybook's own app data.

Calendar access

When you first add or view events, Daybook asks for permission to access your device's Calendar. If you grant it, Daybook reads events from the calendars you choose in Settings and can create, edit, or delete events you make in the app — all directly against the same Calendar database used by Apple's Calendar app. The Analytics dashboard (event counts, time spent, completed vs. upcoming, weekday and per‑calendar breakdowns) is computed entirely on your device from that same data. Daybook never uploads your calendar or event data anywhere.

You can revoke calendar access at any time in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars.

Apple services Daybook relies on

  • EventKit — to read and write events in your existing Calendar accounts, as described above.

Daybook includes no non‑Apple SDKs that collect data, and does not use your location, contacts, camera, microphone, or photo library.

Your control

  • You can delete a day's writing or ink at any time inside the app.
  • Export everything you've written — Settings → Your Data → Export journal writes every written page to a Markdown or JSON file you choose the location of. Handwritten ink is not embedded in the text export; each page reports its ink size instead, and the export states this in the file.
  • Delete everything — Settings → Your Data → Delete all pages permanently removes every page and drawing from this device, after a confirmation. It cannot be undone, so export first if you want a copy.
  • You can remove any event you created through Daybook directly, or via Apple's Calendar app.
  • Revoking calendar access (System Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars) stops Daybook reading your events. It never affects anything you have written.
  • Deleting the app removes its local database and settings from your device.

Children

Daybook does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.
